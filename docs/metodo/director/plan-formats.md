# The formats of `plan.json`

*A procedure of the STL Studio director. The model reads this English version; its Spanish version, `plan-formats.es.md`, has the same parts and is the one people read and approve. A change is written in both. The inbox (`tools/director/bandeja.ts`) says when to read it: in every review, every urgency and every clarification, before writing in the plan.*

## The patch, the mission and its steps

- **It is written with targeted changes, without reading it whole**: what changes goes in a patch, `director/cambios.json` in the project's memory, and `node tools/director/bandeja.ts [--proyecto <id>] --aplicar` merges it into the plan and saves it only if it adds no errors (`validarPlan`). By key (`respuestas`, `revisiones`, `evaluacion`, `ejecutadas`, `reservaCorta`), by id (`misiones`, `propuestas`, `pedidos`, `fases`, `frentes`; `documentos`, by their path), `cambiar` (some fields by id, and an inner list by position: `{ "0": "…", "+": […] }`), `quitar` (by id) and `orden` (the order of a list, without taking anything out: `{ "misiones": ["t_b", "t_a"] }` puts those in that order, in the places they had; to reorder, never take out and add back, because in between what the person already delivered or started is left without its mission and the Studio refuses their decisions); everything else, whole; with null, it goes away (`src/director/parche.ts`). The patch is not kept in the memory: it is overwritten for the next one.
- Mission: `{ "id", "para": "<id de persona>", "fase", "frente", "titulo", "porQue", "pasos": [texto | { "texto", "ir": {…, "etiqueta"}, "hecho"?, "detalle"?, "opciones"?, "varias"?, "hazlo"?, "soloTu"?, "terminal"?, "copiar"? }], "entrega", "documentos"?, "criterios"?, "xp", "vigente"?, "alCierre"?, "despuesDe"?: [ids of missions, proposals or requests], "origen"?: [ids of the notes, proposals, requests or missions it comes from] }`. The `porQue` (why it goes there) is for the director, the inbox and «Lo que sigue»: the mission card does not show it, so the first step carries everything the person needs, without an introduction that repeats it. The experience (`xp`) goes from 10 to 50 according to the effort; 100 XP is a level. A mission born from a note or from something approved says so in `origen`, and each proposal that is executed names in its `ejecutadas` note the commit that did it: this way an idea can be followed end to end, and `bandeja.ts --listo` checks that each thread exists (`origenesQueNoExisten`, `ejecutadasSinCommit`).
- **A round trip is one step**: a check that does an action and then undoes it (turning something off and back on, hiding and showing it) goes in a single step of a single card, which says to try state A, then state B, and then return to the start, with one answer for the whole check; never as two steps or two cards.
- **The step text speaks the interface's language, its labels too**: what is named between «» on screen (a button, a section, a mode) is named as the person's interface shows it, taken from `estudio/src/textos/en.ts` when it is in English («Focus mode», never «Modo enfoque»); the names in «Where each thing is» are the Spanish ones. And a step does not open with «Siguiente paso» or «Next step»: the card already says it, in its language. With the interface in English, `validarPlan` does not let through an open step (queued or of the closing too) that starts with «Siguiente paso» or quotes a Spanish label that is another one in English, and its error names the one to use (`rotulosDelIdioma.ts`). What the person said is still quoted as it is.
- **A step that talks about a button brings its own** (hard rule): the card shows one step at a time, so a step never counts on another step's button («el paso de abajo lleva un botón» left the person with no button in view, because the next step only shows once this one is marked). Any wording that promises a button («el botón de este paso», «con el botón», «oprime el botón de abajo», «ese botón», «this step's button») goes with that step's `ir`; a Studio element is named by its name between «» («Pedir revisión»), and the mouse buttons are not promises. `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close with an open step that promises a button and brings none (`pasosQuePrometenBoton`).
- **A step that asks to choose** has `opciones` (2 to 6 short sentences, without `hecho`): the Studio shows them as buttons in the step, a click leaves the choice in the Inbox (`sobre: { "tipo": "mision", "id", "seccion": "Paso N", "eleccion" }`, signed) and marks the step. It never leads to read a whole document (`ir.doc` without `seccion`): what is read goes in one step and what is chosen, in the next (`validarPlan`; finishing a reading marks only the step that asks to read it). In the inbox it arrives as «Eligió …»: answer it and take it to the plan like any note. For another answer, there is the step's ✉. If the person already answered with the step's ✉, the Studio also offers «Ya te lo dije en mi comentario», which marks the step without choosing an option; in the inbox it arrives with its comment: answer it with what it says. And every step with options carries «Ya lo comenté antes», which marks the step without choosing an option or commenting on it, because the instruction is already in an earlier note: in the inbox it arrives with their latest comments; look for that instruction, follow it and say in the answer which note it was. An option that promises to tell more (with «✉», «te cuento» or «te copio») opens the step's comment when chosen: the choice and what is told arrive together in one note. **If the options are not exclusive** (what to add to something, which of several ideas), also give it `varias: true`: each option is checked and unchecked, and «Listo» delivers them together in a single note, with their `eleccion` in the step's order and joined with « · »; an option that starts with «Ninguna» or «Nada» does not go with the others. It arrives as «Eligió A · B»: answer it option by option. **If one has to wait before answering** (what something published takes to show), give it `esperar` (seconds, from 5 to 900): the Studio shows the countdown, large, and does not show the step's button or its options until it is over (without options, the button is enough); the text does not repeat the wait.
- **A terminal command**: if the step asks to run something in the person's terminal, the command goes in `terminal` (a single line), not in the text. The Studio shows it with «Abrir la terminal con esta orden» (it opens a new window, in the project's folder, with the command running; on Windows, Git Bash) and «Copiar»: nobody has to look for the terminal or where to paste it. The text says what the command does and what is seen at the end. It never goes with `hazlo`.
- **A text to paste somewhere else** (a form outside the Studio, a message to someone): it goes whole in `copiar`, and the Studio shows it in the step with «Copiar el texto», for as long as it is needed. Never only in what a command prints, which is lost when its window closes.
- **Checking a terminal step**: give it `comprobar: { "orden", "espera"? | "vacia"? }` whenever its result can be read: a read-only command (`git log`, `git status`, `git rev-parse`, `git show`, `git ls-files`, `git branch --show-current`, `git config --get`… or `existe <ruta>` / `no existe <ruta>` inside the project; no pipes) and what its output must contain (`espera`) or that it comes out empty (`vacia`). When the step is marked, the Studio runs it in the project's folder, without a model: if it holds, it says «Hecho: lo comprobé»; if not, the step is not marked and it says what it expected and what it found. A checked step already brings its proof: the inbox does not flag it with «Compruébalo». Next to the command, the Studio puts «Ya lo hice», which does the same as «Listo, ya lo hice» in the other steps (with its check, if it has one). **A step that does not work out traps nobody**: next to «Ya lo hice» there is «No me salió», and the notice of a failing check brings «No me sale: sigo con otra». Both deliver the mission as it is, with what happened in its note, and the next one stays in view. The delivery carries the step that did not work out (`Entrega.sinTerminar`), and the inbox shows it as «entregada sin terminar», with the unmarked steps (`pasosSinMarcar`): it is not approved for what was marked; the director does what is missing, if it can and it is approved, or returns it with the step corrected. A delivery like that does not seal the document its step asked to read and that was left unmarked. **A step outside the Studio that nothing checks by itself** (without `hecho` or `comprobar`) also brings «Ya lo hice: compruébalo tú»: it marks the step and launches the director only to check it, like «No lo entiendo» (`bandeja.ts --comprobar`), while the person goes on with their next mission; if it was not met, that step comes back as their first mission, with what was found. That is why such a step says in its `detalle` what to do if what is seen is something else (a page still showing the old version: Ctrl + F5 or wait a few minutes).
- **«Hazlo tú»**: a step with `hazlo` (without `opciones` or `hecho`) brings that button; a dialog says what you will do and, when confirmed, the assignment stays in the Inbox (`sobre: { "tipo": "mision", "id", "seccion": "Paso N", "hazlo": true }`, signed) and the step is marked. **It is not executed instantly**: in the inbox it arrives as «Hazlo tú»; do it in that review, with the project's rules, and answer it with the result and its `destino` (the Studio shows your answer in the step). If in the end it cannot be done, say so and give the step back. When the last step is marked, the mission is delivered; the review is not requested by itself nor offered in the celebration, even if it was the last mission (CLAUDE.md › «Only a person requests a review, and only with a button that says it requests it»). They request it whenever they want, with «Pedir revisión», in the header.
- **«Autorizo», only where the click grants it**: a permission the panel offers (what was denied to you goes there by itself, as the general permission of its kind of action) is granted there, with its checkbox: the step leads to the panel (`{ "vista": "modelo", "ancla": "m-permisos" }`), says which checkbox to mark and has no «Autorizo» options, because the click of an option only leaves a note. «Autorizo» goes only with what the panel does not offer (several commands together, a path without a folder, installing something), with what it changes and costs in view, and that choice is the permission. `validarPlan` does not let through a step with an «Autorizo» option that leads to the panel, names it («Permisos») or copies one of its buttons (`autorizoSinPermiso`).
- **The steps done inside the Studio are marked by themselves**: give them `hecho`, what proves it was done, and the Studio marks them as soon as it sees it (without a model), says «Hecho: lo vi en el Studio» and, if it was the last one, delivers the mission. `{ "que": "meta_fijada" | "meta_reabierta" }` (with `desde` ISO, so as not to count an earlier goal), `{ "que": "leido", "id": "docs/…", "seccion"? }`, `{ "que": "propuesta", "id" }`, `{ "que": "puerta", "id": "<fase>" }`, `{ "que": "nota", "tipo"?, "id"?, "seccion"?, "desde"? }` (a note in the Inbox) or `{ "que": "medicion", "desde": "<ISO now>" }` (a core measurement that started since then and finished; while it runs, its mission goes behind the others, so a mission that only launches it never holds the person, and you read the result in the inbox). **A proposal is read and decided only in «Propuestas»**: the step that leads to it or asks to answer it carries `"ir": { "vista": "propuestas", "ancla": "propuesta-<id>" }`; «La ruta» only summarizes it, and `validarPlan` does not let through a step that sends it anywhere else. If what must be decided fits in the step, it goes in the step, with its `opciones`. What happens outside the Studio (running a command, trying something) goes without `hecho`: it is marked by hand.
- **«No lo entiendo, explícamelo mejor»**: every step brings that button. A click leaves a note on the step (`sobre: { "tipo": "mision", "id", "seccion": "Paso N", "aclarar": true }`) and the Studio launches the director only for that, without the rest of the review (`bandeja.ts --aclarar`): it rewrites **only that step**, without changing its number (the marked ones go by number), with more context in `texto` and, if they are several actions, `detalle` (the list of smaller actions, which the Studio shows inside the step); it answers the note with its `destino` in the mission and closes with `--aclarar --listo`. If it is asked again, go one level deeper: it is the sign that the step was badly written.

## Where each thing is

A step that sends the person to look at or press something says where it is with this map of today's interface, never from memory. If the interface changes, this map changes in the same commit (`index.html`, `estudio/src/app.ts` › `pintarMando`).

- **The left bar**, top to bottom: the little house with «STL Studio», which leads to «Hoy», and the button that folds the menu; the active project, with its list to switch projects; the project's sections («Hoy», «Buzón del director», «Documentos», «La ruta», «Propuestas», «Preguntas» and the rest); **the project line**, the living line with one little face per review, in the free space of that bar, with its magnifier at its bottom right, which opens it large; and at the foot, «Modelo y consumo», who uses the Studio, «ES · EN», the sync and «Qué es STL Studio ↗». The project line is on the left, never at the top.
- **The header**, at the top, above the section: on the left, the project's name › the section · «Fase N de M», and below it the phase's name; on the right, in this order, «Pedir revisión» with its «?», «¿Dónde quedamos?», the focus mode, the light or dark theme and the sound.
- **The director's shortcuts**, at the bottom right, in a column that floats over everything: «Qué está haciendo» (while it reviews), «Modo enfoque», «Niebla de lectura» (only with focus on), «Modo ayuda» (a «?» in a speech bubble, always in view, also in focus mode: crossed out when off, colored when on) and «Comentario», the speech bubble that opens the dialog to write or record to the director (it reaches the Buzón).
- **«Hoy»**: at the top, the thin bar of the phases; below, your mission, one step at a time with its buttons; and below it, folded, «Lo que sigue».
- **«Documentos»**: two columns; on the left, the «Documentos» column with your reading order and, folded, «Documentación del proyecto»; on the right, the document being read, one idea at a time, with its ✉ and its «Entendido» under each idea, and each decision's buttons at the end of its part.
- **The Buzón**: at the top, «Buscar en el Buzón»; below, the filters by type and by how it ended; then the notes that wait and, folded, the ones already answered.
- **The dialogs** open over everything, in the center: «¿Dónde quedamos?», the line in large (with a face's history at one side, in its tabs «Resumen» and «Conversación») and the one to comment.

## The proposals, the requests and the rest

- Proposal: `{ "id", "area", "titulo", "problema", "propuesta", "conecta", "fase", "reemplaza"?: [ids], "cambiaProducto"?, "alCierre"? }`.
- **What is replaced, out**: a request or a proposal that replaces another says so in `reemplaza` (the ids of the requests or proposals it leaves without effect), instead of telling it only in its text. The code takes it out of what is pending (the director's queue, what waits for the closing, what a mission waits for, which then waits for what replaced it), the Studio shows it as «Reemplazado» in «La ruta» and the closing takes it to the archive, where it is searched by its id (`reemplazos`). `validarPlan` does not let through an id that does not exist, one that replaces itself, two that replace the same one or two that replace each other. The same goes for what was decided in the notes: the inbox opens with **«What is in force»**, the decisions of the documents and the options chosen in the steps still in force, one line each (`decisionesVigentes`); a new note on the same decision, or on the same step, replaces the previous one by itself, and the answer to a note that leaves an earlier one without effect (a decision of another part, an option chosen in a step or any note, like a «funciona bien así» that later changed) says so in `reemplaza` (the ids of the notes it replaces): you mark it when answering, the person does nothing more. It leaves what is in force, the Buzón marks it «Reemplazado» and `buscar.ts` says what replaced it (`notasReemplazadas`); `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close if it names something that is not an earlier note of the Buzón, or the same note (`reemplazosRotos`).
- **A note that brings several things is answered in parts**: if it brings, for example, an adjustment and an idea, its answer carries `partes`, two or more, each one `{ "tipo", "texto", "tratamiento", "destino"? }`. The `tipo` is one of `idea` (an idea for later), `ajuste` (changing something that already exists), `falla` (something fails), `pregunta` or `respuesta` (shown as «Comentario»: a comment on what the director said or on the place where it is left), as you read it: if it does not match the one the person chose, you correct it and say so in that part's `texto`. Each part has its treatment and, if it is `integrada`, `aplazada` or `decision`, its `destino`, like the whole answer. The answer's `texto`, `tratamiento` and `destino` are still those of the main thing. The Studio shows each part by itself, under the answer, with its type, its treatment and its «Quedó en»; `validarPlan` does not let through a single part (with only one thing there are no parts), an unknown type or treatment, an empty part or a destination that does not exist, and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close with a part that takes something to the plan without saying where it went (`respuestasSinDestino`).
- **The type of each note**: the person chooses it with one tap when leaving a note (`ajuste`, `respuesta`, shown as «Comentario» and already marked when the note is left from the ✉ of a place or from an option that promises to tell more, `idea`, `falla`, `pregunta`), except where the place already says it (a question, a decision, a chosen option, «Hazlo tú», «No lo entiendo», «Ya lo hice: compruébalo tú»); the inbox shows it with each note. If they chose «Auto (el modelo decide)», the answer carries `tipo`, as you read it, and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close without it (`respuestasSinTipo`); a note from before without one, still unanswered, also gets its `tipo` (the inbox asks for it). Notes answered before stay «Sin tipo», with their own filter in the Inbox.
- What was done ahead: `{ "fase", "frente", "texto" }` (work already done that counts for a future phase).
- The short batch (`reservaCorta` › <person>): its format and when it holds are in CLAUDE.md › «Fewer than ten, only when the route is complete»; the motives `revision` and `cola` no longer hold (`reservaSinRazon`). `ruta` holds with your queue empty; `espera`, when everything in your queue waits only for open missions of the team (each item in the `origen` of at least one), with all of that person's missions your queue waits for in its `lote`.
- The next piece of your queue: `"piezaSiguiente": { "cuando", "id": "<of your queue>", "archivos": [{ "ruta", "parte"? }], "comprueba" }`, written before closing while your queue has something. The Studio, without a model, launches the next review with the parts of those files (a function or a section; the whole file without `parte`) and the procedures the inbox names already inside the assignment, so it starts working instead of reading; with consecutive reviews, where the next one starts by itself, `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close without it (`piezaPorCerrar`).
- Request: `{ "id": "pd_…", "fase", "texto", "notas"?: [ids], "hecho"?: { "cuando", "nota" }, "reemplaza"?: [ids] }` (what was asked in a note, to be done until it has `hecho`). What goes after the goal has `"fase": "despues"`: it shows in «La ruta», at the end of the last phase, in «Después del lanzamiento», with its state, and it holds no gate; the `destino` that leads to it names that last phase.
- A closing mission has `"alCierre": true` (CLAUDE.md › «What closes the phase never goes before approved work»). A request that is done in the closing itself (the installation package, what has to be taken out before building it) also has `"alCierre": true`: it does not hold those missions, because they are the ones that bring it, and the one that needs it waits for it with `despuesDe`. **An accepted proposal can be closing work too** (rewriting the published history just before the upload): with `"alCierre": true` it does not hold the closing missions or a test of the whole product, and it leaves the director's queue; its step goes into the closing mission that brings it (with its «Confirmo» and what is lost in view, if it costs or deletes), and it goes in `ejecutadas` once that step is done.
- **A test of the whole product** (an installation on another computer, a test with someone from outside) has `"pruebaDelTodo": true`, and `validarPlan` does not let through a closing mission with a step on another computer without it. The Studio holds it (`esperaAlCierre`) while anything marked `"cambiaProducto": true` is undone, a request or an accepted proposal **of any phase**, not only of its own; what is done in the closing itself (`alCierre`) does not count. While one such mission is open, each undone request and each accepted proposal says `cambiaProducto`, `true` or `false`, and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close without it (`sinDecirSiCambiaProducto`). If what holds it is in a later phase, its gate cannot be crossed: the route has to move the test and the gate after that work.
- A mission that needs something done first has `"despuesDe"`: the ids of the missions, proposals or requests that precede it. The Studio holds it while any of them is still undone (a mission, until it is delivered; a proposal, until it is executed or discarded; a request, until its `hecho`), shows it in «Lo que sigue» with what it waits for and opens it by itself when it is ready; `validarPlan` does not let through an id that does not exist or a mission before another one of the same person that it needs. This is how the mission to see what the director will do is scheduled, at its point, without bringing it forward.
- Lesson: `{ "titulo", "quien", "que", "fuente", "paraElProyecto" }`, always with a real source.
- The ids of the people are in the `proyecto.json` of its memory › `equipo` (the owner is always `titular`). Each project has its team.
- `validarPlan` (in `src/director/director.ts`) says everything the plan must meet.
