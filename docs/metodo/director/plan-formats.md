# The formats of `plan.json`

*A procedure of the STL Studio director. The model reads this English version; its Spanish version, `plan-formats.es.md`, has the same parts and is the one people read and approve. A change is written in both. The inbox (`tools/director/bandeja.ts`) says when to read it: in every review, every urgency and every clarification, before writing in the plan.*

## The patch, the mission and its steps

- **It is written with targeted changes, without reading it whole**: what changes goes in a patch, `director/cambios.json` in the project's memory, and `node tools/director/bandeja.ts [--proyecto <id>] --aplicar` merges it into the plan and saves it only if it adds no errors (`validarPlan`). By key (`respuestas`, `revisiones`, `evaluacion`, `ejecutadas`, `reservaCorta`), by id (`misiones`, `propuestas`, `pedidos`, `fases`, `frentes`; `documentos`, by their path), `cambiar` (some fields by id, and an inner list by position: `{ "0": "…", "+": […] }`), `quitar` (by id) and `orden` (the order of a list, without taking anything out: `{ "misiones": ["t_b", "t_a"] }` puts those in that order, in the places they had; to reorder, never take out and add back, because in between what the person already delivered or started is left without its mission and the Studio refuses their decisions); everything else, whole; with null, it goes away (`src/director/parche.ts`). The patch is not kept in the memory: it is overwritten for the next one.
- Mission: `{ "id", "para": "<id de persona>", "fase", "frente", "titulo", "porQue", "pasos": [texto | { "texto", "ir": {…, "etiqueta"}, "hecho"?, "detalle"?, "opciones"?, "varias"?, "hazlo"?, "soloTu"?, "terminal"?, "copiar"?, "atajo"? }], "entrega", "documentos"?, "criterios"?, "xp", "vigente"?, "alCierre"?, "despuesDe"?: [ids of missions, proposals or requests], "origen"?: [ids of the notes, proposals, requests or missions it comes from], "configura"?: "repositorio" | "cuenta" | "identidad" | "permisos" }` (`configura`: a mission that completes the project's missing configuration, start.md › «The missing configuration, first»). The `porQue` (why it goes there) is for the director and the inbox: the mission card does not show it, so the first step carries everything the person needs, without an introduction that repeats it. The experience (`xp`) goes from 10 to 50 according to the effort; 100 XP is a level. A mission born from a note or from something approved says so in `origen`, and each proposal that is executed names in its `ejecutadas` note the commit that did it: this way an idea can be followed end to end, and `bandeja.ts --listo` checks that each thread exists (`origenesQueNoExisten`, `ejecutadasSinCommit`). **Unfinished work comes back as a new mission, never as a rework**: with a new id and a forward-looking `titulo` (what to do next, never «redo …»), and its `origen` names the mission it continues. The Studio never says a mission is being redone, so the verdict `"rehacer"` is not written in new plans (it is only read in old ones).
- **A round trip is one step**: a check that does an action and then undoes it (turning something off and back on, hiding and showing it) goes in a single step of a single card, which says to try state A, then state B, and then return to the start, with one answer for the whole check; never as two steps or two cards.
- **The step text speaks the interface's language, its labels too**: what is named between «» on screen (a button, a section, a mode) is named as the person's interface shows it, taken from `estudio/src/textos/en.ts` when it is in English («Focus mode», never «Modo enfoque»); the names in «Where each thing is» are the Spanish ones. And a step does not open with «Siguiente paso» or «Next step»: the card already says it, in its language. With the interface in English, `validarPlan` does not let through an open step (queued or of the closing too) that starts with «Siguiente paso» or quotes a Spanish label that is another one in English, and its error names the one to use (`rotulosDelIdioma.ts`). What the person said is still quoted as it is.
- **The document a step leads to, in their language too** (hard rule, note nmwjl67k8tit): the `ir.doc` of an open step (and the mission's `documentos` list) never leads to a model procedure (`docs/metodo/director/*.md`, with its internal names, like «objetivo») or to a document in a language other than the interface's. The person reads a version written in their language: a director's document, new and short, with what they have to read, or the procedure's version in their language (the `.es.md`, with the interface in Spanish), without internal identifiers. `bandeja.ts --aplicar` and `--listo` do not let it through (`documentosEnOtroIdioma`, inside `textosEnOtroIdioma`).
- **A step that talks about a button brings its own** (hard rule): the card shows one step at a time, so a step never counts on another step's button («el paso de abajo lleva un botón» left the person with no button in view, because the next step only shows once this one is marked). Any wording that promises a button («el botón de este paso», «con el botón», «oprime el botón de abajo», «ese botón», «this step's button») goes with that step's `ir`; a Studio element is named by its name between «» («Pedir revisión»), and the mouse buttons are not promises. `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close with an open step that promises a button and brings none (`pasosQuePrometenBoton`).
- **A step never sends the person to reread what they asked for** (hard rule): they know their own request. The step brings the solution, the proposal and the options to choose, in place, with what is needed to choose; its `ir` never leads to a request (`pedido-<id>`). Inside a mission the Studio does not draw that button, and `validarPlan` does not let through an open mission with one (`llevaAUnPedido`). A request is reached from «La ruta» or from an answer's «Quedó en».
- **A step that sends the person to try something in the project's environment states its conditions** (hard rule): if it sends them to try something in the project's site or app running (a link to localhost, or a text that sends them to try it in the local, development or test environment), it carries `entorno: { "necesita", "comprobado" }`: what must exist there (data, accounts, a server on) and how you checked it in the repository or the environment before writing it; or `entorno: { "necesita", "creaLa": "<mission>" }`, with the earlier mission that creates it, also in its `despuesDe`. Without that, `validarPlan` does not let it through (`pruebaEnEntorno`); if it does not hold, the mission is another one: the one that creates those conditions.
- **A shortcut into the project's app** (a web project whose local door is built: diagnosis.md › «The local door»): the step carries `atajo: { "url", "rol", "como", "arranque"?, "dispositivo"? }`: `url`, the page of the app on this computer (`http://localhost:<port>/<path>`, never another address); `rol`, the role as the app's code knows it; `como`, the role said for the person, in their language («the manager»); `arranque`, the one-line command that starts the app; `dispositivo` (optional), `"telefono"`, `"tableta"` or `"escritorio"`, the view the QA tab opens in, only for a step that needs to check a phone or tablet layout, and only the first time its button opens the tab (without it, or after that, the device the person last chose in that project). A step whose button leads to STL's published page (`ir.url`, which also opens in the QA tab) can carry the same `ir.dispositivo`; on any other button it is not valid. The Studio shows «Open <project> as <como> ↗», which opens that page already signed in, through the project's door, in one tab per project, reused on every press (nmwimp4sxtit); one role at a time, so with another role's session open the button says which one it replaces before it is pressed (nmwi6s3dbtit); closing that tab ends the session. While the app does not answer, the button reads «Start <project> first» and the step shows `arranque` with «Open the terminal with this command»; as soon as the app answers, the button becomes the shortcut again (nmwf5tf1stit). A shortcut is a test in the project's environment, so the step also carries its `entorno`; the text says what to look at on that page, not how to sign in. `validarPlan` does not let through a shortcut to an address that is not local, or without `rol` or `como`.
- **A step that asks to choose** has `opciones` (2 to 6 short sentences, without `hecho`): the Studio shows them as buttons in the step, a click leaves the choice in the Inbox (`sobre: { "tipo": "mision", "id", "seccion": "Paso N", "eleccion" }`, signed) and marks the step. It never leads to read a whole document (`ir.doc` without `seccion`): what is read goes in one step and what is chosen, in the next (`validarPlan`; finishing a reading marks only the step that asks to read it). In the inbox it arrives as «Eligió …»: answer it and take it to the plan like any note. For another answer, there is the step's ✉. If the person already answered with the step's ✉, the Studio also offers «Ya te lo dije en mi comentario», which marks the step without choosing an option; in the inbox it arrives with its comment: answer it with what it says. And every step with options carries «Ya lo comenté antes», which marks the step without choosing an option or commenting on it, because the instruction is already in an earlier note: in the inbox it arrives with their latest comments; look for that instruction, follow it and say in the answer which note it was. An option that promises to tell more (with «✉», «te cuento» or «te copio») opens the step's comment when chosen: the choice and what is told arrive together in one note. **If the options are not exclusive** (what to add to something, which of several ideas), also give it `varias: true`: each option is checked and unchecked, and «Listo» delivers them together in a single note, with their `eleccion` in the step's order and joined with « · »; an option that starts with «Ninguna» or «Nada» does not go with the others. It arrives as «Eligió A · B»: answer it option by option. **If one has to wait before answering** (what something published takes to show), give it `esperar` (seconds, from 5 to 900): the Studio shows the countdown, large, and does not show the step's button or its options until it is over (without options, the button is enough); the text does not repeat the wait.
- **A terminal command**: if the step asks to run something on the person's computer, the command goes in `terminal` (a single line), not in the text. The Studio shows it with «Correr aquí» first (the Studio runs it itself, hidden, in the project's folder; the first time of each kind of action the step asks «Hazlo ahora», «Hazlo ahora, y siempre en este proyecto» or «No»; its result and its folded output stay under the command, a step that finished well marks itself, and one that fails leaves its error note in the Inbox), then «Abrir la terminal con esta orden» (a new window, for what asks the person to type) and «Copiar». So what the person used to give you with «Hazlo tú» because it was only a command is written as a step with `terminal`, which runs on their click, and no step asks to click and then press a key in a window. The text says what the command does and what is seen at the end, naming «Correr aquí» by its label in the interface's language. It never goes with `hazlo`.
- **A text to paste somewhere else** (a form outside the Studio, a message to someone): it goes whole in `copiar`, and the Studio shows it in the step with «Copiar el texto», for as long as it is needed. Never only in what a command prints, which is lost when its window closes.
- **Checking a terminal step**: give it `comprobar: { "orden", "espera"? | "vacia"? }` whenever its result can be read: a read-only command (`git log`, `git status`, `git rev-parse`, `git show`, `git ls-files`, `git branch --show-current`, `git config --get`… or `existe <ruta>` / `no existe <ruta>` inside the project; no pipes) and what its output must contain (`espera`) or that it comes out empty (`vacia`). When the step is marked, the Studio runs it in the project's folder, without a model: if it holds, it says «Hecho: lo comprobé»; if not, the step is not marked and it says what it expected and what it found. A checked step already brings its proof: the inbox does not flag it with «Compruébalo». Next to the command, the Studio puts «Ya lo hice», which does the same as «Listo, ya lo hice» in the other steps (with its check, if it has one). **A step that does not work out traps nobody**: next to «Ya lo hice» there is «No me salió», and the notice of a failing check brings «No me sale: sigo con otra». Both deliver the mission as it is, with what happened in its note, and the next one stays in view. The delivery carries the step that did not work out (`Entrega.sinTerminar`), and the inbox shows it as «entregada sin terminar», with the unmarked steps (`pasosSinMarcar`): the director does what is missing, if it can and it is approved, or gives it back as a new mission with the step corrected; the delivered one is approved for what was done, with all its experience points, and the new one carries its own (nmwk5m5p1tit). A delivery like that does not seal the document its step asked to read and that was left unmarked. **A step outside the Studio that nothing checks by itself** (without `hecho` or `comprobar`) also brings «Ya lo hice: compruébalo tú»: it marks the step and launches the director only to check it, like «No lo entiendo» (`bandeja.ts --comprobar`), while the person goes on with their next mission; if it was not met, that step comes back as their first mission, with what was found. That is why such a step says in its `detalle` what to do if what is seen is something else (a page that may still show the old version: one short line, «If you still see the old version, refresh the cache», or wait a few minutes; never a reload key by habit, in any step; and never that line in a step that opens in the QA tab, with `atajo` or a link to STL's published page, because that tab never uses the cache: `pasoQaConRecordatorioDeCache`).
- **«Hazlo tú»**: a step with `hazlo` (without `opciones` or `hecho`) brings that button; a dialog says what you will do and, when confirmed, the assignment stays in the Inbox (`sobre: { "tipo": "mision", "id", "seccion": "Paso N", "hazlo": true }`, signed) and the step is marked. **It is not executed instantly**: in the inbox it arrives as «Hazlo tú»; do it in that review, with the project's rules, and answer it with the result and its `destino` (the Studio shows your answer in the step). If in the end it cannot be done, say so and give the step back. When the last step is marked, the mission is delivered; the review is not requested by itself nor offered in the celebration, even if it was the last mission (CLAUDE.md › «Only a person requests a review, and only with a button that says it requests it»). They request it whenever they want, with «Pedir revisión», in the header.
- **A permission is asked for at the moment, not in a step**: what the director needs during a review it just tries, and the Studio's banner asks the person at that moment, with «Only this time», «Always, in this project», «Always, in all projects» and «I do not authorize it», while the review waits, paused (CLAUDE.md › «What needs a permission is asked for at that moment»); only after a «no» does it do the rest and say what was missing. No step asks for a permission the banner can ask, nor leads to the panel to mark a checkbox for it. «Autorizo» goes only with what happens outside the Studio (installing something only the person can run, signing in to an account), with what it changes and costs in view, and that choice is the permission. `validarPlan` does not let through a step with an «Autorizo» option that leads to the panel, names it («Permisos») or copies one of its buttons (`autorizoSinPermiso`). **A confirmation that records the permission** (note nmwlai8g8tit: an urgency that explicitly asked to push or deploy): the step brings `autoriza: { "reglas": ["Bash(<command>:*)"] }`, an option that starts with «I authorize» («Autorizo» in Spanish) and another that does not; choosing it records those rules in the panel for that project, as a marked checkbox, so the urgency or the next review uses them without asking again (`erroresDeAutoriza`, `reglasQueAutoriza`).
- **The steps done inside the Studio are marked by themselves**: give them `hecho`, what proves it was done, and the Studio marks them as soon as it sees it (without a model), says «Hecho: lo vi en el Studio» and, if it was the last one, delivers the mission. `{ "que": "meta_fijada" | "meta_reabierta" }` (with `desde` ISO, so as not to count an earlier goal), `{ "que": "leido", "id": "docs/…", "seccion"? }`, `{ "que": "propuesta", "id" }`, `{ "que": "puerta", "id": "<fase>" }`, `{ "que": "nota", "tipo"?, "id"?, "seccion"?, "desde"? }` (a note in the Inbox) or `{ "que": "medicion", "desde": "<ISO now>" }` (a core measurement that started since then and finished; while it runs, its mission goes behind the others, so a mission that only launches it never holds the person, and you read the result in the inbox). **A proposal is read and decided only in «Propuestas»**: the step that leads to it or asks to answer it carries `"ir": { "vista": "propuestas", "ancla": "propuesta-<id>" }`; «La ruta» only summarizes it, and `validarPlan` does not let through a step that sends it anywhere else. If what must be decided fits in the step, it goes in the step, with its `opciones`. What happens outside the Studio (running a command, trying something) goes without `hecho`: it is marked by hand.
- **«No lo entiendo, explícamelo mejor»**: every step brings that button. A click leaves a note on the step (`sobre: { "tipo": "mision", "id", "seccion": "Paso N", "aclarar": true }`) and the Studio launches the director only for that, without the rest of the review (`bandeja.ts --aclarar`): it rewrites **only that step**, without changing its number (the marked ones go by number), with more context in `texto` and, if they are several actions, `detalle` (the list of smaller actions, which the Studio shows inside the step); it answers the note with its `destino` in the mission and closes with `--aclarar --listo`. If it is asked again, go one level deeper: it is the sign that the step was badly written.

## Where each thing is

A step that sends the person to look at or press something says where it is with this map of today's interface, never from memory. If the interface changes, this map changes in the same commit (`index.html`, `estudio/src/app.ts` › `pintarMando`).

- **The left bar**, top to bottom: the little house with «STL Studio», which leads to «Hoy», and the button that folds the menu; the active project, with its list to switch projects; the project's sections («Hoy», «Buzón del director», «Documentos», «La ruta» and the rest; «Propuestas» left: each proposal is a point of the line in «Hoy»; «Preguntas» left the Studio: a question is a note of the type «Pregunta», left with «Comentario»); **the project line**, the living line with one little face per review, in the free space of that bar, with its magnifier at its bottom right, which opens it large; and at the foot, «Modelo y consumo», «ES · EN», the line of the last backup («Último respaldo», «Sin respaldo» or «El respaldo falló»; a click opens its small window or leads to where it is connected) and «Qué es STL ↗», with «Nota para el Studio» on its same line (only with the Studio registered and another project open: it leaves the note in the Studio's Buzón, saying which project it came from, and launches nothing). The project line is on the left, never at the top.
- **The header**, at the top, above the section: on the left, the project's name › the section and, below, its goal in a few words (with the plan still in phases, «Fase N de M» and the phase's name instead); on the right, in this order, «Pedir revisión» with its «?», «¿Dónde quedamos?», the focus mode, the light or dark theme and the sound.
- **The director's shortcuts**, at the bottom right, in a column that floats over everything: «Qué está haciendo» (while it reviews), «Modo enfoque», «Niebla de lectura» (only with focus on), «Modo ayuda» (a «?» in a speech bubble, always in view, also in focus mode: crossed out when off, colored when on) and «Comentario» (to write to the Studio itself from another project there is only «Nota para el Studio», at the foot of the left bar), the speech bubble that opens the dialog to write or record to the director (it reaches the Buzón), in aquamarine, the Studio's accent. There is no chat in the Studio (nmwiklx70tit).
- **«Hoy»**: at the top, the line to the goal (from «Hoy» to the flag, which says how many points are left; each pending item a point in its color, without text under it: its instant bubble says what it is, whole; the current one bigger, the missions delivered in this round ticked (a click shows what was said and chosen in each step); twelve in view, the ticked ones included, and the rest in «y N más»; a click on a point opens its card below the line, inside «Hoy», with «Volver a mi misión»: a proposal is accepted or discarded there, and with every point done the flag's card asks to confirm «Meta cumplida» with the unmarked items of its checklist in view, then offers «Editar la meta» and, once edited, «Pedir la revisión»; beside the goal in the header, «Editar la meta» is always at hand), and under it the tabs «Tu misión» and «Historia»; below, your mission, one step at a time with its buttons. «Lo que sigue» and the «Propuestas» section left the Studio: a step that leads to `{ "vista": "propuestas", "ancla": "propuesta-<id>" }` opens that proposal's card on the line. While the director attends an urgency, and after it until the person opens it or hands in a mission, one line above the mission says so (with «Ver qué cambió» when it ended); meanwhile the buttons that move a mission forward are disabled.
- **«Documentos»**: two columns; on the left, the «Documentos» column with your reading order and, folded, «Documentación del proyecto»; on the right, the document being read, one idea at a time, with its ✉ and its «Entendido» under each idea, and each decision's buttons at the end of its part.
- **The Buzón**: at the top, «Buscar en el Buzón»; below, the filters by type and by how it ended; then the notes that wait and, folded, the ones already answered.
- **The dialogs** open over everything, in the center: «¿Dónde quedamos?», the line in large (with a face's history at one side, in its tabs «Resumen» and «Conversación») and the one to comment.

## The proposals, the requests and the rest

- Proposal: `{ "id", "area", "titulo", "problema", "propuesta", "conecta", "fase", "reemplaza"?: [ids], "cambiaProducto"?, "alCierre"? }`.
- **What is replaced, out**: a request or a proposal that replaces another says so in `reemplaza` (the ids of the requests or proposals it leaves without effect), instead of telling it only in its text. The code takes it out of what is pending (the director's queue, what waits for the closing, what a mission waits for, which then waits for what replaced it), the Studio shows it as «Reemplazado» in «La ruta» and the closing takes it to the archive, where it is searched by its id (`reemplazos`). `validarPlan` does not let through an id that does not exist, one that replaces itself, two that replace the same one or two that replace each other. The same goes for what was decided in the notes: the inbox opens with **«What is in force»**, the decisions of the documents and the options chosen in the steps still in force, one line each (`decisionesVigentes`); a new note on the same decision, or on the same step, replaces the previous one by itself, and the answer to a note that leaves an earlier one without effect (a decision of another part, an option chosen in a step or any note, like a «funciona bien así» that later changed) says so in `reemplaza` (the ids of the notes it replaces): you mark it when answering, the person does nothing more. It leaves what is in force, the Buzón marks it «Reemplazado» and `buscar.ts` says what replaced it (`notasReemplazadas`); `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close if it names something that is not an earlier note of the Buzón, or the same note (`reemplazosRotos`).
- **A note that brings several things is answered in parts**: if it brings, for example, an adjustment and an idea, its answer carries `partes`, two or more, each one `{ "tipo", "texto", "tratamiento", "destino"? }`. The `tipo` is one of `idea` (an idea for later), `ajuste` (changing something that already exists), `falla` (something fails), `pregunta` or `respuesta` (shown as «Comentario»: a comment on what the director said or on the place where it is left), as you read it: if it does not match the one the person chose, you correct it and say so in that part's `texto`. Each part has its treatment and, if it is `integrada`, `aplazada` or `decision`, its `destino`, like the whole answer. The answer's `texto`, `tratamiento` and `destino` are still those of the main thing. The Studio shows each part by itself, under the answer, with its type, its treatment and its «Quedó en»; `validarPlan` does not let through a single part (with only one thing there are no parts), an unknown type or treatment, an empty part or a destination that does not exist, and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close with a part that takes something to the plan without saying where it went (`respuestasSinDestino`).
- **The type of each note**: the person chooses it with one tap when leaving a note (`ajuste`, `respuesta`, shown as «Comentario» and already marked when the note is left from the ✉ of a place or from an option that promises to tell more, `idea`, `falla`, `pregunta`), except where the place already says it (a question, a decision, a chosen option, «Hazlo tú», «No lo entiendo», «Ya lo hice: compruébalo tú»); the inbox shows it with each note. If they chose «Auto (el modelo decide)», the answer carries `tipo`, as you read it, and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close without it (`respuestasSinTipo`); a note from before without one, still unanswered, also gets its `tipo` (the inbox asks for it). Notes answered before without a type have no filter of their own: they show only under «All» in the Inbox.
- What was done ahead: `{ "fase", "frente", "texto" }` (work already done that counts for a future phase).
- The short batch (`reservaCorta` › <person>): its format and when it holds are in CLAUDE.md › «Fewer than ten, only when the route is complete»; the motives `revision` and `cola` no longer hold (`reservaSinRazon`). `ruta` holds with your queue empty; `espera`, when everything in your queue waits only for the person's open missions (each item in the `origen` of at least one), with all of that person's missions your queue waits for in its `lote`.
- The next piece of your queue: `"piezaSiguiente": { "cuando", "id": "<of your queue>", "archivos": [{ "ruta", "parte"? }], "comprueba" }`, written before closing while your queue has something. The Studio, without a model, launches the next review with the parts of those files (a function or a section; the whole file without `parte`) and the procedures the inbox names already inside the assignment, so it starts working instead of reading; if it is missing, `bandeja.ts --listo` reminds you without stopping the closing (`piezaPorCerrar`).
- The next review's limit: `"tramo": { "cuando", "minutos" }`, only if what comes next is a long execution: from 60 to 90 minutes; without it, 60 (`limiteDelTramo`). It holds only for the next review. There is one review at a time: nothing launches the next one when a review closes.
- Request: `{ "id": "pd_…", "fase", "texto", "notas"?: [ids], "hecho"?: { "cuando", "nota" }, "reemplaza"?: [ids] }` (what was asked in a note, to be done until it has `hecho`). What goes after the goal has `"fase": "despues"`: it shows in «La ruta», at the end of the last phase, in «Después del lanzamiento», with its state, and it holds no gate; the `destino` that leads to it names that last phase.
- **A phase that leaves the route**: it goes out of `fases` (with its planned missions) and its id goes into `"fasesRetiradas": [ids]`, in the same patch. The history keeps its buttons: an answer or an approved mission that leads to it is not an error, and the Studio takes it to the last phase, to its request or proposal where it shows today, or to «Después del lanzamiento» (`destinoVigente`). An open mission cannot lead there, and an id that is both in the route and withdrawn does not pass `validarPlan`.
- **The goal, outside the phases** (a plan on one line): the goal's checklist (`criterios`), the parts it moves (`frentes`), its gate and its plan live in `linea.meta` (the «meta» phase without its id), and `fases` is empty; you keep it, with a patch by key (`{ "linea": { "meta": {…} } }`), which leaves the line's history as it is. In memory it is the current phase again, so «Goal reached» and `evaluacion` work as before. A plan that still keeps the phase moves it with `bandeja.ts --aplicar --meta-fuera` (and `--en-linea` already leaves it outside); if a patch brings the phase back, the phase wins and the closing saves it again in `linea.meta`.
- A closing mission has `"alCierre": true` (CLAUDE.md › «What closes the goal never goes before approved work»). A request that is done in the closing itself (the installation package, what has to be taken out before building it) also has `"alCierre": true`: it does not hold those missions, because they are the ones that bring it, and the one that needs it waits for it with `despuesDe`. **An accepted proposal can be closing work too** (rewriting the published history just before the upload): with `"alCierre": true` it does not hold the closing missions or a test of the whole product, and it leaves the director's queue; its step goes into the closing mission that brings it (with its «Confirmo» and what is lost in view, if it costs or deletes), and it goes in `ejecutadas` once that step is done.
- **A test of the whole product** (an installation on another computer, a test with someone from outside) has `"pruebaDelTodo": true`, and `validarPlan` does not let through a closing mission with a step on another computer without it. The Studio holds it (`esperaAlCierre`) while anything marked `"cambiaProducto": true` is undone, a request or an accepted proposal **of any phase**, not only of its own; what is done in the closing itself (`alCierre`) does not count. While one such mission is open, each undone request and each accepted proposal says `cambiaProducto`, `true` or `false`, and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close without it (`sinDecirSiCambiaProducto`). If what holds it is in a later phase, its gate cannot be crossed: the route has to move the test and the gate after that work.
- A mission that needs something done first has `"despuesDe"`: the ids of the missions, proposals or requests that precede it. The Studio holds it while any of them is still undone (a mission, until it is delivered; a proposal, until it is executed or discarded; a request, until its `hecho`), shows its point on the line with what it waits for and opens it by itself when it is ready; `validarPlan` does not let through an id that does not exist or a mission before another one of the same person that it needs. This is how the mission to see what the director will do is scheduled, at its point, without bringing it forward.
- Lesson: `{ "titulo", "quien", "que", "fuente", "paraElProyecto" }`, always with a real source.
- The person's id is in the `proyecto.json` of its memory › `equipo` (always `titular`).
- `validarPlan` (in `src/director/director.ts`) says everything the plan must meet.
