# The review cycle

*A procedure of the STL Studio director. The model reads this English version; its Spanish version, `review-cycle.es.md`, has the same parts and is the one people read and approve. A change is written in both. The inbox (`tools/director/bandeja.ts`) says when to read it: in every review.*

## The steps (en marcha)

With what the inbox lists:
1. **Deliveries**: in `revisiones`, per mission: `{ "cuando": "<ISO now>", "veredicto": "aprobada" | "rehacer", "comentario": "…" }`. Concrete: what went well, what is missing. **What is marked is not enough: check it.** A step that happens outside the Studio (running a command, trying something, setting up an account) is marked by hand and the Studio cannot see whether it was done: the inbox flags it with «Compruébalo». Before approving, look for the evidence (the repository, the tool, what was left) and say in the comment what you checked; if there is none, do not take it as done: give the step back and say what you did not find. The same in every open mission with steps marked by hand.
2. **Notes**: in `respuestas`, per note id: `{ "cuando": "<ISO>", "texto": "…", "tratamiento": "…" }`. Every note has an answer, with your recommendation, and **its treatment** (STL Methodology, step 7): `integrada` (it was incorporated into the project), `rechazada` (it is not incorporated, and the answer says why), `aplazada` (it is kept for a later phase: say which, and write it there, in its planned missions or in a proposal of that phase; what lives only in an answer goes to the archive and nothing shows it again; that is why the inbox lists, in the current phase, each answer «aplazada» for it or for an earlier one that has neither its request nor its proposal, `aplazadasSinPedido`, until it has one), `decision` (there is an ambiguity or a conflict you do not resolve: raise it as a decision in the Studio) or `informada` (it only informed or asked). **It is chosen, not built** (request pd_formulario): the inbox gives with each note the treatments that fit it (`tratamientosDeNota`: a «Hazlo tú» or a decision in a document is not just informed; what the person chose or a question is not rejected) and its destinations at hand (`destinosDeNota`), and `--listo` does not close with a treatment that does not fit (`tratamientosQueNoCaben`). **The double check:** if it is `integrada`, `aplazada` or `decision`, or if it answers a decision in a document (unless you reject it), the answer says where it went in the plan with its `destino`, like a step's button (`{ "vista": "misiones", "ancla": "mision-<id>", "etiqueta": "…" }`, a proposal, a phase, or `{ "doc", "seccion" }` of a document on the list): the Studio shows it as «Quedó en: …», `validarPlan` checks that the place exists and `bandeja.ts --listo` does not close the review while one is missing. **What is asked stays as a request** (the owner's voice note of 22 October 2026: their requests lived only in the text of an answer or in a sentence of the planned missions, without a state, and were lost from view): each note that asks for work and is not done in that same review, nor is already a proposal or a mission, leaves its request in `plan.pedidos`, with its phase, when it is answered, and the answer's `destino` leads to it (`{ "vista": "ruta", "fase": "<its phase>", "ancla": "pedido-<id>" }`, never to the whole phase, where it cannot be found; `--listo` does not close with an integrated or postponed answer whose button leads to «La ruta» without its anchor: `pedido-<id>`, `propuesta-<id>` or, if the phase plan changed, `r-plan`); when it is done, it gets `hecho` (when and how: the commit). The Studio shows them in «La ruta» with their state, the inbox lists them and the mission that closes the phase waits for them, as for an accepted proposal. Read the notes **all together** before answering: two ideas in different notes can be a single one. **The urgent ones** (`urgente`: a checkbox, without a separate reason; read the note to judge how urgent it is) are a request for priority, not an order: **you still decide the route**. Normally they arrive already attended: the Studio launched you only for them (`bandeja.ts --urgente`, which says what to do). If it truly goes first and it is approved or asked for in the Studio, do it now and leave the result as a report in `resumen.avanzamos` (with «Revisa lo urgente» only if it has to be checked); if not, explain which mission or criterion must go first and why. Those that reach the review (because the Studio could not launch you) are put first by the inbox: treat them the same. Always say, in one sentence, whether it was truly urgent.
3. **Criteria**: in `evaluacion`, those already met with what was delivered: `{ "fecha", "estado": "cumplido" | "no_aplica", "nota": "why" }`.
4. **What comes next gathers everything said**: before closing, take the feedback to every document nobody has reviewed yet («Documentos por revisar» in the inbox), integrated where it belongs and not as an appendix. Never ask to review a document that does not include what was already said. **And what was already done is not read again**: the director knows at every moment what was done and what was not, and how that affects what comes next. For each document or step to be read, check whether what it explains, proposes or asks to decide was already done, decided or approved some other way (a note, a «Hazlo tú», several iterations, what was achieved); if so, take it out of the mission and say so in the answer: asking to confirm what is already done is going backwards. The inbox watches it: if since the last review there was work (commits in the repository, «Hazlo tú», notes on a mission), it lists each open mission and `--listo` does not close until the director, after checking it, gives it `vigente` (the ISO date of now); also the ones it writes in that review. The same with **everything pending in the plan**, not only the missions: an unanswered proposal whose work was already done or is already a mission leaves the plan (the owner is not asked to discard it: checking it is the director's job), an accepted one that was already executed goes in `ejecutadas`, and the planned missions of each phase (`fases[].misiones`) are updated with what was done; if something changes the route, the following missions change with it. **And the code requires it** (the owner's voice notes of 22 October 2026: «that the future plan not be static»): if since the last review there was work (commits in the repository, also those of that same review, or «Hazlo tú»), `--listo` does not close until the director, after crossing with what was done each accepted proposal not executed, each request not done and the planned missions of each phase, writes down what it checked and what changed in `plan.alDia` (`{ "cuando", "nota" }`; `pendientePorComprobar`). The pending items that a commit since the last review names by their id or truly resembles are listed by the inbox in «Puede que ya esté hecho», with that evidence, and `--listo` does not close until each one is done or its id is in that note (`puedeQueYaEsteHecho`, `pistasSinAtender`). That is why **each person has only the next document to read**: the others are written when their turn comes, with whatever has happened by then (the inbox warns if there is more than one). What comes later lives in the plan (the phases, their missions and the proposals), not in documents written in advance.
5. **Decide again each person's next mission** with what was learned: if the one coming is no longer the right one, change it. One mission at a time.
6. `mensaje`: the director's comments on this review, in one to three bullets (one per topic, with its topic in bold), without pending items or requests and **without repeating what was achieved** (that goes in `resumen.avanzamos`; see «Dónde quedamos»). It is read in «¿Dónde quedamos?», after what was achieved. **Do not describe there the mission or what each one does today**: the Studio takes each person to their mission, and what they have to do goes in it (`personas` is no longer shown). If it is time, `boletin` (grades A to F per subject; grade the **focus**, not the quantity).
7. If the diagnosis of a front changed, update it with the date.
8. **Questions**: the inbox lists them separately («Preguntas sin respuesta»). They are notes with `sobre: { "tipo": "pregunta" }` that the owner writes down when they come up, so as not to interrupt their work, and they pile up like the comments until your review. The Studio shows them in «Preguntas», **apart from the comments**, and your answers are read **as a report**: one per review, together. Answer them in `respuestas` like any note, but written to be read on their own:
   - **The direct answer first** (yes, no, how much, which, when), then the why, in a few sentences. You can use Markdown (bold, lists, links): the report shows it formatted.
   - Complete: no need to open anything else to understand it. If it refers to something in the Studio or the repository, name it precisely.
   - `tratamiento`: almost always `informada`. If the answer reveals work to do, say so in the answer and take it to a proposal or a mission (`integrada`); if it asks the owner to decide, `decision`, with the decision in its place.
   - Read the questions and the notes together: a question can be answered with the same as another note.
   - **Conversations**: on an answer the owner can keep asking or commenting; that note carries in `sobre.id` the question it follows, and the Studio shows them together, folded. The inbox gives you what was already said in the conversation: answer in that context, without repeating what you already answered.

## «Dónde quedamos» (the summary of each project)

The first thing one asks when coming back to a project is «how are we doing, where did we leave off?». The Studio shows it to the owner every time they switch to that project, with a button straight to their current mission, and adds what is new since their last visit. The director writes it in `plan.resumen` at the end of **every** review of that project:
```json
"resumen": { "fecha": "<ISO>",
  "avanzamos": ["What was done, one line per advance, with its concrete result if there is one: newest first, at most 6"],
  "quedamos": "Where we left off: the state of the project (what was achieved, the phase, the goal), in one or two sentences. No pending items.",
  "alerta": "Only if there is a deadline or an active risk, in one line" }
```
Written for someone who comes from another project and remembers nothing: concrete and without jargon. The Studio shows it together with the `mensaje`, in two parts that **do not repeat each other**:
- **«Lo que avanzamos»** (`avanzamos`) is what was done: the only place where it is told. **One line per advance**, never two about the same thing: if a new advance continues or replaces an earlier one on the same topic, the old line goes. A line already written **is not rewritten** (each rewritten line shows up again to the owner as new): it is left as is or removed. The concrete result (what was measured, tested or delivered) goes inside its line, not separately (`resultados` is no longer written).
- **«Comentarios del director»** (`mensaje`) is what the director has to say that is not an advance: why it decided something, what it could not do, a warning, what is worth knowing. It never tells again what is already in `avanzamos` or what the previous comment already said (`bandeja.ts --listo` does not close if a sentence of the message repeats a line of what was achieved or a sentence of the saved message: rewritten or lengthened, the Studio would show it again as new). **It goes in bullets, one per topic**, each one starting with its topic in bold (`- **Sobre el candado:** …`): the Studio shows each bullet as a separate line, which is read and commented on by itself. If there is nothing to comment on, one sentence is enough. It serves to **recover the context** (what happened and where the project is), **not for pending items**: not what comes next, not what is missing, not decisions to make. Only the mission card and the floating director talk about that; the Studio leads to the mission with a button. The `alerta` is also seen in the list of projects: use it sparingly (a date, a risk in production), never to push work.

## Validate and close

- `npm test` (the template and the rules) and that the inbox shows no errors in the route. An invalid `plan.json` shows as an error in the Studio.
- `node tools/director/bandeja.ts --listo` is the last thing that touches the plan: the Studio shows what it finds at that moment, and a change afterwards (even a sentence of the message) reaches the owner as another version of the same thing. If something must change, it is changed and `--listo` is run again before the commit.
- A local commit in the project's memory (branch `stl`), only with what is yours: `plan.json`, the archive (`director/archivo/`) if there is one, the map (`mapa.json`), `docs/` and the X-ray (the inbox gives the command; «Director (<id>): <what you reviewed>»). The team's work is saved by the Studio.
- **Minimal chat**: in the chat, one or two lines («Revisión hecha; todo está en el Estudio») plus only what the owner needs to know and is not there: a problem, something that could not be done, an action only they can take outside the Studio. Do not repeat in the chat what you already wrote in the Studio.
