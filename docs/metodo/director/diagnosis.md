# The diagnosis (existing project)

*A procedure of the STL Studio director. The model reads this English version; its Spanish version, `diagnosis.es.md`, has the same parts and is the one people read and approve. A change is written in both. The inbox (`tools/director/bandeja.ts`) says when to read it: at the `diagnostico` stage of an existing project and for each project of the diagnosis queue.*

## The steps

1. `node tools/director/diagnosticar.ts`: the X-ray (history, structure, languages, tools, tests, documentation, hot spots, debt). They are facts, not judgment.
2. **Really read the repository**: the README and the documentation, the manifests, the entry points, the key code of each area, the tests, the CI, the latest commits. Also read its `proyecto.json` (the context and what the owner wants to achieve) and the notes in the Inbox. If it is large, use reading subagents and put their findings together. **Also research what people who know that kind of project recommend** (an app, a thesis, a site, a product).
3. **Define the fronts**: the work areas that make sense for the project (not one per folder), each one with its `rutas` (prefixes of the project's repository), to measure the effort. Keep the `direccion` front.
4. Write in its memory (`.stl/director/plan.json`; the inbox gives the path):
   - `diagnostico`: `fecha` (today), `resumen` (3 to 6 paragraphs: what it really is, what is solid, what is missing, what risks there are, where the effort goes), `frentes` (per front: `nota` from 0 to 5 = how ready it is for what the owner wants, not how much was worked; `hecho`, `falta`, `veredicto` in one sentence), `fuentes` (what you read) and `estrategia` (`texto`: the strategy for this kind of project according to the experts, applied to what you found, in one or two paragraphs; `fuentes`: the real ones, with their address). The Studio shows it in Project, in «El diagnóstico del director»: the summary, the readiest and the weakest fronts in one paragraph, the strategy and the document one click away.
   - `objetivos`: **exactly 2 candidates**, in this order: first **the shortest one that leaves something truly valuable**, then **the best fit** for what the owner told and what the diagnosis found. Each with `texto` (what is achieved), `exito` (how it is checked: a number or a verifiable fact), `porQue` (why that one, from the diagnosis), `riesgo` and, if it applies, `horizonte` (a milestone, never an imposed date). The goal screen shows only these two, editable, with «Confirmar la meta y trazar la ruta», and no field for a goal of the owner's own: what they want to change, they change in one of the two. If both would be the same goal, say so in the second one's `porQue` and make it differ in reach.
   - `frentes`: the new ones, and in the `arranque` phase each one on hold (`"nivel": "espera"`) except `direccion`.
   - `detectado`: what you already know about the project, so the owner does not have to write it: `contexto` (what it is, for whom and where it stands, in one paragraph) and `meta` (the goal you suggest, in one sentence), **written in the language the owner chose in the Studio's interface** (the inbox's «Team» line says it), whatever language the repository is in. The Studio shows it in «Project» › «What it is», marked «Detected»: in the empty field if the owner told nothing, or beside their own words if they did, with «Use this» to replace theirs in one click; nothing they said leaves view without that click. Write it even when the owner already told a context: it is their choice.
   - `archivosClave`: the project's key files (`[{ "ruta", "para" }]`, up to 8, the ones someone has to know to understand it, with what each one is for in one short sentence, in the owner's language). The Studio shows them in «Project» › «What it is» and in «Documents».
   - The GitHub account is not yours to write: while the project has no approved account, the Studio suggests the repository's owner from its remote (or, without a GitHub remote, the active gh session), «Detected, not saved», for the owner to save with one click.
   - `memoria`, inside `diagnostico`: how up to date the project's own memory is (below, «The project's memory»).
   - Whether the project gets a local door into its app, and how it would open (below, «The local door»): described, never built, in the diagnosis.
   - `evaluacion`: `a_contexto` if the owner already told the context.
   - A document `docs/director/diagnostico.md` in its memory (its format: `documents.md`) and its entry in `documentos`.
   - **If the owner has no goal yet** (they only want the diagnosis and to run it with STL), what would improve the project goes as `propuestas` (suggested missions), never as missions: they decide which ones. With the goal set, the route is drawn.
   - The owner's mission: read the diagnosis and comment on it part by part, with its button to the document, and set their goal (button to `{ "vista": "proyecto", "ancla": "p-objetivo" }`). Criteria: `a_leido`. Its steps lead by the hand (CLAUDE.md › «Lead by the hand»): in the diagnosis, what to look for (what is solid, what is missing, the candidates for the goal), «Entendido» on each idea, each decision answered at the end of its part, and «← Volver a tu misión» to come back; in «La meta», how it is set (change what they want in one of the two goals and press «Confirmar la meta y trazar la ruta», which sets it and requests the review that draws the route).
   - `mensaje` and, if you want, a first `boletin`.
   - **No alarm in «Dónde quedamos»**: the review that delivers a diagnosis does not write `resumen.alerta` (the red ⚠ box). The risks it found go inside the diagnosis itself (its `resumen` and the document), told in plain words as part of the report, not as an alarm (note nmwb8rwsntit).
5. Validate and close (`review-cycle.md` › «Validate and close»).

## The project's memory

The project may bring its own memory: instructions for AI agents (`CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.github/copilot-instructions.md`, a `memory-bank/`…) and documents that tell its state (the README, a roadmap, its plans). If that memory fell behind the code, or is repeated in files that no longer say the same, a diagnosis built on it starts from a point that is no longer the real one. The X-ray measures it without the model, in «La memoria del proyecto»: how much the code changed after each file's last change, what it cites that no longer exists, the repeated instructions and Claude Code's own memory of that project on this computer.

1. While reading, check those signals: a cited path may be something planned or written the project's own way, and a project with many commits a day may be a little behind and still tell the truth. Where the memory and the code differ, the code is right, and the summary says so.
2. Write `diagnostico.memoria`: `nivel` (`al_dia`, `algo_atrasada`, `atrasada`, `muy_atrasada` or `sin_memoria`; the X-ray's, unless your reading says otherwise, and then the summary says why) and `texto` (one or two sentences for the owner: which files, how far behind, and what it means for them). The Studio shows it in «El diagnóstico del director».
3. The diagnosis document has a short part on it, its last one, named in the person's language («Your project's memory»): what is behind, with an example or two, and what it means.
4. **If it is behind** (`algo_atrasada`, `atrasada` or `muy_atrasada`), offer the deep check at the end of the diagnosis, as the proposal `p_verificar_memoria` (phase `arranque`, area `direccion`): STL compares each thing the memory says with the code, corrects its own diagnosis with what it finds, and shows what is outdated in the project's files, so the owner decides whether STL updates them. Its `problema` gives the level and where it shows; its `propuesta` asks it plainly, in the person's language, along these lines: «Do you want STL to check your project's memory against its code? It takes one more review and more of your plan's usage than an ordinary one, but we start from the real state and not from outdated information.» The last step of the owner's mission leads to it (`{ "vista": "propuestas", "ancla": "propuesta-p_verificar_memoria" }`, with `hecho`: `{ "que": "propuesta", "id": "p_verificar_memoria" }`). `validarPlan` does not let a memory that is behind through without its proposal.
5. If it is up to date, or there is none, there is no proposal: the part says so. Accepted, it enters your queue and the inbox names its procedure (`memory-check.md`).

## The local door

A local door lets a mission's step open the project's app already signed in as a user of a given role, with the page in view, so the person looks at what the step asks without typing an account or a password («Open IDG as the manager ↗»). While receiving the project, the director decides whether it gets one and describes it; nothing is stored, and no account or password is ever written anywhere (nmwejfjritit).

1. **Only web projects** (nmwgj1ymrtit). Read the repository to tell: a web project runs on this computer as a site or an app with an address (a development server, its command and its port) and has a sign-in (routes or a layer that creates the session, and users with roles in its own database). A thesis, a library, a command-line tool, a mobile app without a local web build or a site without sign-in gets no door: the diagnosis says so in one sentence, and its missions take the person to the file or the command instead, never to an «Open» button that cannot work.
2. **What the diagnosis describes, for a web project** (nmwejgrvbtit): how the app starts locally (the command and the address), how its sign-in creates a session (the file and the mechanism), which roles exist and where their users live, and how the door would create that session by itself from the project's own code and database. It goes in the diagnosis document, in a short part named in the person's language («The shortcuts into your app»), and in one sentence of the summary, so the first missions can already count on it.
3. **Its three locks, always** (nmwejhse4tit): a switch file outside git, without which the door does not answer (created when the door is built; while the Studio runs, the door is on); requests only from this same machine; and the Studio linking only to that local address, refusing any other. Whoever directs the project is the only one who can use it, on their own computer.
4. **A project whose own rules ask first** (nmwf3oq3htit): if its rules (its `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md` or equivalent) require a confirmation before a code change, the door arrives as a one-click proposal in its phase, with what it adds and its three locks in view, and no question is asked anywhere else; accepted, it is built like any approved work. Otherwise, handing the project over is the permission to build it (nmwejfjritit).
5. **When it is built** (nmwf3ptu3tit): never during the diagnosis. The door is built, with its test, in the review that writes the first mission that needs to take the person into the app, in that project's repository and with its rules. A project handed over before the doors existed has its door described from its repository in its next review, with no new diagnosis, and built the same way (nmwgmtf6atit). It does not depend on remembering it: the inbox flags it in every review of that project until it is adopted (`src/director/funciones.ts`; nmwjn980ctit).
6. **What the door answers, so the Studio can use it** (`src/director/atajo.ts`): two routes in the project's app. `/__stl/puerta?rol=<role>&ir=<path>` creates the session of a user of that role and takes the person to that page; if the app's database on this computer has no user of that role, it creates one test user of that role, clearly named as the Studio's, only there, and reuses it every time after (nmwi6rbcktit); a session of another role is replaced, one role at a time (nmwi6s3dbtit). `/__stl/puerta/salir` ends that session, which the Studio calls when the shortcut's tab closes (nmwflqauvtit). Both answer only while the switch file `.stl-puerta` exists in the repository's root (added to that project's `.gitignore` when the door is built) and only to requests from this same machine, and both are left out of any build that is published. The Studio's own route checks the same locks before it links to the door, and its missions reach it with the step's `atajo` (plan-formats.md).

## The AI history

If the project brings `historialIA` (previous conversations with AI agents), read it **once**, in the diagnosis, with a reading subagent (CLAUDE.md › «Earlier conversations with pending work»): look for what was left half done or waiting for approval. Never answer in those conversations or continue them.
